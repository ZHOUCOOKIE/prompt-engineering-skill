## Known conflicts

Where official sources disagree with each other, the resolution used here and why.

### 1. "Prompt engineering best practices for 2026" (2025-11) vs. the living docs

That post recommends three things the current documentation contradicts:

| The post says | The living docs say | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Prefill the assistant response as an advanced technique | Prefill on the last assistant turn returns 400 from Claude 4.6 onward | **Docs.** Use Structured Outputs or an explicit instruction |
| XML tags are "less necessary with modern models" | Structuring prompts with XML tags is a recommended technique; on this repo's reading (an observed pattern, not a documented rule), the official snippets tag blocks that need to function as one named unit | **Docs** |
| "Heavy-handed role prompting is often unnecessary" | Giving Claude a role focuses behavior; even one sentence helps | **Docs**, with the post's useful corollary: stating the *perspective* is often more effective than attaching an identity label |

The post's framing sections and its "common mistakes" list remain useful.

### 2. Examples: include 3–5, or remove them

Both are official and they are not in conflict — they address different situations. Locking a format, tone, or structure calls for examples; an agent system prompt that needs judgment does better without them. **Examples constrain**; use them when constraint is what you want.

### 3. Verification: Opus 5 says remove it, the Fable page says add it

Also not a conflict, on this repo's reading. What the Opus 5 page tells you to remove is explicit verification instructions — including "use a subagent to verify" — and legacy harness steps that add separate verification. What the Fable 5 page's long-run guidance adds is *a separate, fresh-context verifier subagent* run on an interval — which the same page notes tends to outperform self-critique. The skill therefore keeps the two apart by model: no verification instructions on Opus 5 unless measured, periodic fresh-context verification on Fable long runs. The Fable 5.1 page neither restates nor retracts the instruction; it carries over on that page's statement that existing Fable 5 prompts should perform well on Fable 5.1 without changes.

**Opus 5.5 adds a third position, and it is a genuine gap rather than a conflict.** The Opus 5.5 pages say nothing about over-verification either way, while the best practices page's general advice — `Ask Claude to self-check` — still favours a self-check instruction and names Claude Opus 5 as its only exception. So the repo does not assert the Opus 5 finding about Opus 5.5: §3.4 tells you not to add self-verification reflexively and to treat removing an inherited one as a measurement. The Opus 5.5 page's checklist and `a separate, smaller model check the conversation against it at each end of turn` address a different problem — turns that end before the task is done — so they check completion, not output quality, and do not bear on whether to remove or add verification.

### 4. The Opus 5 effort starting point — not a conflict, a documentation change

Worth recording in full, because it is the clearest demonstration of why this repo defers to the live docs.

During review, two independent passes reported that the Opus 5 recommendation was "start at `high`." Three direct fetches of the Opus 5 page returned instead that `xhigh` "remains the recommended starting point" for coding and agentic work, and the effort page at that time had no Opus 5 section at all. The reports were recorded here as unreproducible.

They were not wrong. On re-verification the page reads:

> **Efficiency at lower effort:** `low` and `medium` effort produce strong quality at a fraction of the tokens and latency of higher settings. Start with the default (`high`) and adjust based on your evals: use `low` and `medium` liberally as your primary control for token cost and response time wherever quality holds, and step up to `xhigh` for demanding coding and agentic work.

The phrase "remains the recommended starting point" no longer appears anywhere on the page, and the effort page has since gained per-model sections for both Opus 5 and Fable 5. The documentation changed underneath the snapshot.

**The guidance in this repo then followed the current page: start Opus 5 and Fable 5 at the default `high`, and step up to `xhigh` for demanding coding and agentic work.** (On Opus 5.5 the default has since moved to `medium`; see §2 and the 2026-09-22 section below.) The practical difference from the earlier wording is real — an ordinary coding task now starts at `high`, not `xhigh`.

The lesson generalizes past this one parameter: **a snapshot of fast-moving documentation is wrong by default, not by exception.** For anything you are using to plan cost or latency, confirm it against the live page.

## A note on scope

The stated scope is Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1. Both predecessors are now on the official legacy list, and snippets published only on their pages are kept on each successor page's carry-over sentence and labeled at the point of use:

- **From the Claude Opus 5 page:** 4.2–4.5 and 4.10, the self-correction block in `models.md`, and the thinking-disabled combined instruction. The basis and labeling are described in the 2026-09-22 section above.

Two further snippets, and the upfront-specification passage in `for-claude-code.md`, come from the **Claude Sonnet 5** page, and this is deliberate rather than an oversight:

- **4.16 (code review: coverage over filtering).** The Opus 5 page independently diagnoses the behavior and prescribes the fix in prose — `ask it to report everything and filter in a separate pass instead` — but publishes no wording for it. The Sonnet 5 page publishes wording for the same prescription. The block is model-agnostic prompt text. The Opus 5.5 page does not restate the literal-instruction behavior; it does report that early testers found stronger code review than on Opus 5, with more bugs caught and fewer false alarms.
- **4.25 (front-end aesthetics).** The short `frontend_aesthetics` block and the four-visual-directions prompt both come from the Sonnet 5 page. The prompting best practices page carries a **different and much longer** `frontend_aesthetics` block; they are not interchangeable, and this repo ships the short one.

The justification used to include that Sonnet 5 was a current model. It no longer is: Sonnet 5 has since joined the legacy list (Claude Sonnet 5.5 shipped on 2026-09-28), and the Sonnet 5.5 page republishes neither block. The Sonnet 5 page is still live and still carries both verbatim, and what keeps them here is the rest of the argument — both are model-agnostic prompt text, 4.16 fills in wording for a move the Opus 5 page prescribes, and 4.25 is the short form of a block the best practices page carries at length. Both entries carry a provenance note at the point of use. If you would rather not mix pages, drop 4.16 and 4.25, the copy of 4.25's visual-directions block in §3.8, and the two Sonnet 5 sentences under "Say it all at once" in `for-claude-code.md` (the Opus 5 sentence there stands on its own), and remove the pointers to 4.16 and 4.25 in SKILL.md (§3.8, the checklist and the symptom table), `models.md` and `for-claude-code.md`. Nothing else depends on them.

Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Haiku 5.5 themselves are outside the scope. The skill names them where the official selection matrix would send a task to them, and says where its hard rules do not transfer (§2), but takes no guidance from their pages.
